
# Meaning and Scope of Ethics

1. Morality 
	- refers to set of standard an individual person or society uses to judge whether an act is good or bad, whether someone is virtuous or not, or whether we ought to do this or that.
2. Ethics
	- The word "Ethics" is sometimes used to refer to one's set of moral beliefs and practices. Strictly speaking, however, it refers to the discipline that examines the moral standards of an individual or society. Being a branch of philosophy that studies the nature of morality, it is sometimes also called moral philosophy.


## 3 General Areas of Ethics

1. Metaethics
	- looks into nature, meaning, scope and foundations of moral values, beliefs and judgements.
	- Asks morality objective or relative? Is morality based on reason, emotions, intuition or facts? What are moral persons? What does it mean to be morally accountable?
	- More philosophical part of ethics
2. Normative Ethics
	- Concerned with the formulation of moral standards, rules or principles to determine right from wrong from wrong conduct of ways of life worth pursuing
	- determine good or bad
	- 3 considerations about acts: that they lead to consequences; that they follow or violate rules; that they are done by persons with character traits
	- Theories under this are generally classified into consequentialism, deontology and virtue ethics.
3. Applied Ethics
	- Examines particular moral issues occurring in both personal and social spheres
	- Moral permissibility of actions and practices in specific areas of human concern
	- business ethics, bioethics, computer ethics


## Descriptive Ethics

Meta, norma, applied are areas of ethics taken as a philosophical study of morality. A non-philosophical study of morality which seeks to objectively record and present how people in a certain community make moral judgements or develop their capacity for such is called descriptive ethics.

## Moral Statements as Normative Statements
- Moral statements are normative or prescriptive, not descriptive or factual.
- They are concerned with how things should be rather than what things are

## Normative Statements in General
- While we appeal to results of research, we appeal to certain stanrdard in validating normative statements
- Morality, aesthetics, grammar, legality and etiquette

## Characteristics of Moral Standards

Moral Standards and Other Normative Standards
- Moral standards are often confused with other normative standards also concerned with "good" or "proper" behavior such as:
1. Standards of Etiquette: based on culture or conventional practices
2. Legal Standards: based on governmental laws
3. Religious Standards: based on religious law

What may be acceptable for these other normative standards may not be acceptable for moral standards due to the characteristics of moral standards.
